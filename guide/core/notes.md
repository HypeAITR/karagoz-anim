# Your own notes

You start every task without memory. The guide is the same for every production and changes rarely. What you learn
on **this** production — a word that made the image model draw the wrong thing, the reason for a retake, a
character's shirt text that must never be mirrored — is lost when your task ends unless you write it down. Nobody
writes your notes for you; the orchestrator sends notes and retakes, you decide what is worth keeping.

## Where

`notes/<your department>/` in your working folder (e.g. `notes/layout/`). It is copied in before your task and
saved back after, so your department's next run starts with it. Create it if it does not exist. You are given the
lessons that match your task; all your notes are in the folder if you need more.

## What

| File | What | Form |
|---|---|---|
| `LESSONS.md` | something that went wrong and what you now do differently | numbered, newest last; **Symptom → Cause → What I do now**; a `tags:` line (the section ids or situations it is about, e.g. `tags: handover, props`) |
| `DECISIONS.md` | a choice the script and inputs did not settle, and why | one line each, with the shot and run |
| `PRODUCTION.md` | facts about this production that matter for your work and are written nowhere else: a character's look or limits, a place, a prop, how the director likes things | one short paragraph per subject |

Example lesson:

```
3. Symptom: in the handover the receiver already held the glass in an inbetween before contact (two glasses on screen).
   Cause: I described her closing hand but not that it was still empty.
   What I do now: in every inbetween before contact I write "right hand open and empty".
   tags: handover, props
```

Keep them true. If a lesson turns out wrong, correct it and say why; do not delete it silently. When two lessons say
the same thing, merge them. If one of your own lessons contradicts the guide, the guide wins until a human changes
it — write the contradiction down. (A retake note from the orchestrator is different: see "Your brief" in the
studio core.)

## When

Before you start: read the notes you were given. Before you finish: add what this task taught you. A retake almost
always deserves a lesson; a smooth task may deserve nothing.

## Never

Secrets, keys, personal information about real people, copies of other files (point to them instead).
