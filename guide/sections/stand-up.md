# Standing up from a seat

Standing up is one continuous move:

1. (anticipation) the torso leans forward over the feet, hips still on the seat, hands pushing on the knees or the
   seat edge;
2. the hips lift off the seat while the head moves forward and up; the knees are still bent;
3. the legs straighten, the torso comes upright, the head rises to its standing height;
4. (settle) standing.

The hips **only rise** from the sitting key to the standing key. The head first moves forward with the lean (it
may dip a little there), then **only rises**. At no point are the hips lower than on
the seat: a body that drops into a squat before standing reads as falling and then popping up. Make the sitting pose
and the standing pose the keys, and put the rising in the inbetweens (four is a good start). Do not make a held key
out of step 2.

Why this section exists: in an early slice the middle of a stand-up was a `crouch` key. The image model drew it as a
deep squat, lower than sitting; the timer held it for 17 frames; then the next drawing was already fully standing.
On screen the character sank, froze, and popped up.
